*Published November 9, 2026*

Hard water is not a safety problem. It is a problem for your pipes, your appliances, your soap, and your skin. Here is how to read the numbers and what to do with them.

## What the numbers mean

Hardness is the amount of calcium and magnesium in your water. You will see it listed two ways. Grains per gallon is what softeners use. Parts per million is what many water reports use. One grain per gallon is about 17.1 parts per million.

| Grains per gallon | What it is usually called |
|---|---|
| Under 1 | Soft |
| 1 to 3.5 | Slightly hard |
| 3.5 to 7 | Moderately hard |
| 7 to 10.5 | Hard |
| Over 10.5 | Very hard |

The city report lists total hardness of 250 parts per million for its own plant. It lists 168 parts per million for Lewis & Clark water. That puts plant water well into the very hard range, and Lewis & Clark water in the hard range.

## How to find your own number

1. **Find your supplier.** Look at your water bill. It tells you if you are on city water or a rural system. If you have no bill, you likely have a private well.
2. **Read the annual report.** Water suppliers publish a report every year. Many list hardness.
3. **Test at your tap.** Your own number is the one that counts. A [water test](/services/water-testing) also shows whether your softener is doing its job.

## What hard water does to your home

- It leaves white crust on faucets and shower heads.
- It leaves spots on glasses and shower doors.
- It builds up inside your water heater, which can make it work harder.
- It keeps soap from lathering, so you use more of it.
- It can leave your skin feeling dry and your laundry feeling stiff.

## What you can do about it

A [water softener](/services/water-softeners) takes out the minerals that make water hard. It is the usual answer for a whole home. A [reverse osmosis system](/services/reverse-osmosis-drinking-water) improves drinking water at one tap, and it works well alongside a softener. A [whole house filter](/services/whole-house-water-filtration) helps with taste and odor, but it does not soften your water.

## Using the numbers on your softener

If your softener asks for a hardness setting, use the number from a test at your tap. If you do not have one, the city publishes a softener setting in its report. Check the current report, because numbers can change from year to year. If your home also has iron, your setting may need adjusting.

## Common questions

### Is hard water bad for me?

Hard water is not considered a health risk. The main problems are scale, soap, and comfort.

### Why does my hardness number change?

Sioux Falls gets water from its own plant and from Lewis & Clark. The two have different hardness, so your number can shift.

### Do towns near Sioux Falls have the same water?

Not always. Your supplier may be a town system, a rural water system, or your own well. Check your bill and your annual water report.
